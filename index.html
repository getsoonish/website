Why I made Soonish

I have ADHD, and to-do apps never stuck for me. Seeing everything I needed to do at once made me freeze. The red badge number kept climbing until I stopped looking, and eventually I'd delete the app and feel disappointed in myself for not sticking with something that was meant to help.

I'd tried the big to-do apps too. They looked great, but I spent more time sorting my list than doing it, and the list only got longer.

So I built the opposite. The first version picked three things a day. Then I lived with it, and on hard days even three was too much. I caught myself marking days off that weren't really days off, just so my streak wouldn't break, without doing anything at all. Three was still the same wall, just smaller.

So now Soonish starts with one. You drop everything into the Scratchpad, and each day it shows you one thing. When that starts to feel easy, it asks if you'd like two, then three, but it never asks for more than three, and you can always go back.

Soonish nudges you gently, then goes quiet once you're done. On a day off, or a day with nothing on your list, it doesn't nudge you at all. And if a day slips by, Soonish asks the next morning whether it was a day off. It only asks about yesterday, and nobody gets shamed for it.

Instead of a red number on the icon, a widget on your Home Screen shows what's next, and you can check it off right there. You earn badges for what you get done, and you're never shamed for what you don't.

I made it for people like me, but a lot of people get overwhelmed by how most to-do apps work and just need to take it one day, one task, at a time. I hope it helps you the way it's helped me.
